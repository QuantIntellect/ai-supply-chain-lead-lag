# MU - AI demand notes from earnings calls, 2024-09-25 to 2026-09-30
(7 calls in sample; no calls before 2024-09, none between 2025-06 and 2025-12 or between 2026-03 and 2026-09; the 2026-09-30 call is a Q&A-only post-earnings call, so its headline figures come from the transcript's summary block)

- 2024-09-25: FY24 revenue $25.1bn (+62%); HBM reached "several hundred millions" of revenue in FY24 and is sold out for CY24 and CY25 with pricing set; HBM TAM seen rising from ~$4bn (CY23) to >$25bn (CY25). Data-center demand exceeds leading-edge supply. Record FQ1 guide $8.7bn +/-$200M; FY25 capex raised to ~mid-30s% of revenue (FY24 capex $8.1bn). Early warning: PC customers had built inventory ahead of price increases.
- 2024-12-18: AI side accelerating: HBM revenue more than doubled QoQ, HBM3E designed into NVIDIA Blackwell B200/GB200, shipments started to a 2nd large HBM customer (3rd in CQ1), HBM TAM raised to >$30bn for 2025 (from $25bn). Largest data-center customer was ~13% of revenue.
- 2024-12-18: Non-AI correction: FQ2 guide cut to $7.9bn +/-$200M (FQ1 was a record $8.7bn) on a "more pronounced" consumer inventory reduction and a temporary pause in data-center SSD purchases; NAND capex and wafer output cut; FY25 capex ~$14bn +/-$500M, mostly for HBM.
- 2025-03-20: HBM passed $1bn per quarter (+50% QoQ); 2025 HBM TAM raised again to >$35bn; HBM3E 12-high designed into GB300; talks on CY26 HBM volumes under way. Data-center NAND demand eased on customer inventory; consumer inventories normalizing. FQ3 guide $8.8bn +/-$200M.
- 2025-06-25: HBM at a >$6bn annualized run rate, sold out for 2025; volume shipments to 4 HBM customers (GPU and ASIC); designed into AMD MI355X. CY26 HBM bit growth expected to "significantly exceed" DRAM. Record FQ4 guide $10.7bn +/-$300M (FQ3 $9.3bn). Additional $30bn of US investment (2nd Idaho fab) within a $200bn plan.
- 2025-12-17: The shortage peaks. FQ1 FY26 revenue $13.6bn (+57% YoY); FQ2 guide $18.7bn +/-$400M at ~68% gross margin; DRAM prices +20% QoQ. All CY26 HBM, including HBM4, is sold out on price and volume. Micron can meet only ~50% to two-thirds of several key customers' medium-term demand, and expects industry supply to stay "substantially short" of demand.
- 2025-12-17: FY26 capex raised to ~$20bn (from $18bn) for HBM and 1-gamma; HBM TAM now ~$100bn by 2028, two years earlier than before (~40% CAGR from ~$35bn in 2025). DRAM and NAND bit shipments ~+20% in CY26.
- 2026-03-18: FQ2 revenue $23.9bn (+196% YoY) at a 75% gross margin. FQ3 guide $33.5bn +/-$750M (~81% gross margin). Signed its first five-year strategic customer agreement (SCA). FY26 capex >$25bn, with a further step-up in FY27 (construction +>$10bn YoY); Tongluo fab bought from Powerchip (shipments FY28). HBM4 is in volume shipment for NVIDIA Vera Rubin. Data-center NAND revenue more than doubled QoQ. Supply to stay very tight beyond 2026; the price-sensitive consumer market is softening.
- 2026-09-30: FQ4 revenue $54.23bn; FQ1 FY27 guide $60-63bn. More than 75% of FY27 shipments are committed. 26 SCAs (10 new) cover ~35% of DRAM bit volume through 2030. HBM pricing raised significantly for CY27. Clean-room space is now the binding constraint and Micron has "no line of sight" to supply-demand balance. Co-designing custom HBM4E (NV HBM) with NVIDIA; NAND industry growth ~mid-20% in CY27-28.
Current state (latest call): 2026-09-30. Micron's memory is still sold out and allocated: >75% of FY27 shipments are committed, SCAs run to 2030, CY27 HBM prices are higher, and the FQ1 FY27 guide is $60-63bn after $54.23bn in FQ4. Supply is limited by clean rooms, not demand, with no visible balance point.
