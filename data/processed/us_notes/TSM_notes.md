# TSM - AI demand notes from earnings calls, 2022-01-13 to 2026-10-15
(14 calls in sample; no calls for Apr-2022, Apr-2023, Apr/Jul-2025 or Jan/Apr-2026, so the 2025 and early-2026 guidance steps are only partly visible)

- 2022-01-13: Peak-cycle tightness. Guided 2022 revenue growth of mid-to-high 20s% (USD) and a Q1 revenue guide of $16.6-17.2bn (+7.4% QoQ). Set the 2022 capex budget at $40-44bn, with 70-80% going to advanced nodes. Said capacity would stay "tight throughout 2022" and named HPC (CPU, GPU, AI accelerators) as the largest source of incremental growth.
- 2022-07-14: Raised 2022 growth to mid-30% and guided Q3 to $19.8-20.6bn (+11.2% QoQ). This call also flagged the smartphone/PC/consumer inventory correction, expected to last "at least into the first half of 2023", although "customers' demand continue to exceed our ability to supply". Some 2022 capex was pushed out into 2023.
- 2022-10-13: Digestion reaches TSMC. Guided Q4 flattish at $19.9-20.7bn (+0.4% QoQ) and cut 2022 capex to about $36bn from $40-44bn. N7/N6 utilization is falling while customers adjust inventory through 1H23. N3 demand still exceeds supply because of tool-delivery delays. Reiterated the 15-20% long-term revenue CAGR.
- 2023-01-12: Trough guide. Q1'23 guided to $16.7-17.5bn (-14.2% QoQ), with 2023 framed as only "a slight growth year". 2023 capex set at $32-36bn, versus $36.3bn spent in 2022. Supply-chain inventory to "reduce sharply" through 1H23, while N3 stays fully utilized.
- 2023-07-20: AI gets sized, but the year is cut. Cut 2023 to about -10% (foundry industry down mid-teens) as customers control inventory into 4Q23, and moved capex toward the low end of $32-36bn. Sized server AI processors at about 6% of revenue, growing at close to 50% CAGR over 5 years to low-teens % of revenue. CoWoS is "very tight", and TSMC cannot fulfil 100% of customer needs.
- 2023-10-19: Recovery starts. Q4 guided to $18.8-19.6bn (+11.1% QoQ); 2023 capex about $32bn; N3 at 6% of wafer revenue. TSMC is working to more than double CoWoS capacity but is "limited by my supplier's capability", and expects output to more than double from 2023 to 2024.
- 2024-01-18: Guided 2024 to low-to-mid 20s% growth, with growth every quarter; Q1 guide $18-18.8bn. 2024 capex of $28-32bn (2023 actual $30.45bn) means capex growth "has begun to level off". N3 revenue is expected to more than triple, and CoWoS/SoIC to grow at more than 50% CAGR. "Almost all the AI innovators are working with TSMC."
- 2024-04-18: Server AI processor revenue to more than double in 2024 to low-teens % of revenue, then grow at a 50% CAGR to more than 20% of revenue by 2028. Q2 guide $19.6-20.4bn (+27.6% YoY). CoWoS capacity is "probably more than double this year", and the second Arizona fab was upgraded to N2 for AI demand.
- 2024-07-18: Raised 2024 to "slightly above mid-20s" percent and guided Q3 to $22.4-23.2bn (+32% YoY). Capex narrowed up to $30-32bn (from $28-32bn). CoWoS is "very, very tight" through 2025, with balance hoped for "2025 or 2026", and capacity is planned to grow at a 60% CAGR.
- 2024-10-17: Raised the AI outlook: AI processor revenue to "more than triple" in 2024 to mid-teens % of revenue (April said "more than double"). Q4 guided to $26.1-26.9bn (+35% YoY). "Our customers demand far exceed our ability to supply." CoWoS capacity more than doubled in 2024, will "probably double again", and is "still not enough".
- 2025-01-16: Capex step-up. 2025 capex of $38-42bn versus $29.8bn spent in 2024. 2025 revenue growth guided to close to mid-20s%; Q1 guide $25-25.8bn (+34.7% YoY). AI accelerators were close to mid-teens % of 2024 revenue, are expected to double in 2025, and to grow at a mid-40% CAGR over 2024-29. TSMC "cannot even meet customers' need".
- 2025-10-16: Raised 2025 growth to close to mid-30s% and narrowed capex up to $40-42bn. Q4 guided to $32.2-33.4bn (+22% YoY). AI demand is "more stronger than we thought 3 months ago". Front-end and back-end AI capacity are "very tight", and TSMC is still working to narrow the CoWoS demand/supply gap.
- 2026-07-16: Raised 2026 growth to slightly above 40%. Raised 2026 capex to $60-64bn (from $52-56bn) and added $100bn more for Arizona. Q3 guided to $44.6-45.8bn (+37% YoY); HPC was 66% of Q2 revenue. Agentic AI is driving a CPU resurgence on top of accelerators. Packaging capacity "is so tight that now it's limiting my customers' growth"; consumer demand is weak.
- 2026-10-15: Raised 2026 growth to about 44%. Q4 guided to $52.3-54.3bn (+14% QoQ, +58% YoY); HPC was 68% of Q3 revenue. Reiterated 2026 capex of $60-64bn and said 2026-2028 capex will be "even more significantly higher than the past three years". N2 capacity is to more than double in 2027. The demand/supply gap is "still quite big", tool supply is tight, and consumer/price-sensitive segments are hurt by rising component prices.
Current state (latest call): 2026-10-15. Demand still runs well ahead of supply in leading-edge wafers and CoWoS. 2026 revenue growth is guided to about 44% (USD), with Q4 at $52.3-54.3bn (+58% YoY). Capex is $60-64bn in 2026 and set to rise further through 2028. The only soft spot is consumer and price-sensitive end markets, hit by component inflation.
