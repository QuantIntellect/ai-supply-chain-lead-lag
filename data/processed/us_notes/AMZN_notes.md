# AMZN — demand notes from earnings calls (2022–2026)

Coverage: 17 calls, 2022-02-03 to 2026-07-30 (actual call dates). Missing: Q1-25 and Q2-25. Through 2024, capex means "capital investments" (capex plus equipment finance leases). From 2025-10-30 onward, management quotes "cash CapEx".

- 2022-02-03: 2022 capex, including equipment finance leases, was guided up y/y. AWS saw no "bottlenecks on the capacity side".
- 2022-04-28 to 2022-10-27: Retail overbuild. Amazon had "excess capacity in our fulfillment and transportation network", cut operations capex, and held 2022 capital investments at about $60bn, flat with 2021, with fulfillment and transport down about $10bn and infrastructure about half the total. AWS customers were optimizing ("working with customers to lower their bills"). AWS growth slowed from 33% to 28% while AWS backlog grew 60%+.
- 2023-02-02 to 2023-10-26: AWS growth troughed at +20% → +16% → +12% → +12%. 2023 capital investments were guided below $59bn, then "slightly more than $50bn", then about $50bn (actual: $48.4bn). On 2023-08-03 management said optimizations were "moderating" and that AWS and generative-AI infrastructure capex was rising. A strategic collaboration with Anthropic followed on 2023-10-26.
- 2024-02-01: AWS reaccelerated to +13%. 2024 capex was guided up on AWS and generative AI, and generative AI was expected to drive "tens of billions" of revenue over several years.
- 2024-04-30 to 2024-08-01: 2024 capex was guided to "meaningfully increase" (Q1: $14bn; H1: $30.5bn, with H2 higher). AI reached a "multibillion-dollar" run-rate. AWS backlog was $156.6bn (+19% y/y) and the AWS run-rate passed $105bn.
- 2024-10-31: 2024 capex was guided to about $75bn, and the CEO said "we'll spend more on that in 2025". He also said "we have more demand that we could fulfill if we had even more capacity". AWS grew 19.1% to a $110bn run-rate, and AI was growing at triple digits.
- 2025-02-06: Q4-24 capital investments were $26.3bn, and that run-rate was called "reasonably representative" of 2025 (implies about $105bn; the analyst framed it as about $100bn). Management said AWS "could be growing faster" without capacity limits, including power constraints. AWS grew 19% to a $115bn run-rate.
- 2025-10-30: 2025 cash capex was guided to about $125bn, with 2026 higher. AWS grew 20.2% to a $132bn run-rate. Backlog was $200bn, and October's unannounced deals together exceeded all of Q3's deal volume. Amazon added 3.8GW in 12 months and expects to double capacity again by 2027. Project Rainier is running about 500,000 Trainium2 chips for Anthropic, rising past 1 million by year-end. Trainium2 is fully subscribed and grew 150% q/q.
- 2026-02-05: 2026 capex was guided to about $200bn. AWS grew 24%, its fastest in 13 quarters, to a $142bn run-rate. Backlog was $244bn (+40% y/y). Amazon added 1.2GW in Q4 (3.99GW over the last twelve months). The chips business (Graviton plus Trainium) passed a $10bn run-rate. An OpenAI agreement was signed in November 2025.
- 2026-04-29: Backlog was $364bn, excluding the new Anthropic deal of more than $100bn. AWS grew 28%, its fastest in 15 quarters, and "there is just not enough capacity for the amount of demand". Anthropic and OpenAI have made multi-gigawatt Trainium commitments, and the chips run-rate passed $20bn. Memory costs have "skyrocketed".
- 2026-07-30: 2026 cash capex was raised to about $220bn from about $200bn on higher memory costs. Backlog reached $496bn (triple-digit growth), and AWS grew 36.7%, its fastest in 18 quarters. Amazon will "still not have enough capacity" in 2026, and probably not in 2027 either. 2027 capacity is "largely reserved", with some 2028 capacity already reserved. AI and chips run-rates each passed $25bn.

Current state (latest call, 2026-07-30): AWS growth is at a multi-year high (+36.7%), backlog is $496bn, 2026 cash capex is about $220bn (partly memory inflation), and capacity remains short through 2027, with most of 2027 already reserved.
