# NBIS — demand notes from earnings calls (2022–2026)

Coverage note: only Q1-25, Q1-26 and Q2-26 calls are in the data (Q2-25 to Q4-25 are missing).

- 2025-05-20 (Q1-25): revenue up nearly 400% y/y; April run-rate revenue (ARR) $310m, with year-end-2025 ARR guided at $750m–1bn. 2025 capex ~$2bn (raised). More than 100 MW deployed in 2025 and a pipeline of more than 1 GW mid-term. One of five NVIDIA reference Cloud Partners and an early Blackwell Ultra (GB300) cloud.
- 2026-05-13 (Q1-26): AI business ARR $1.9bn (from $1.25bn at end-Q4). Signed a $27bn 5-year Meta contract ($12bn dedicated compute plus a $15bn capacity option). All capacity commitments to Microsoft and Meta delivered. 2026 capex raised to $20–25bn from $16–20bn on 2027 visibility, with sites, power and customer commitments secured for 2027. 2026 ARR guide $7–9bn. Sold out again, with 4+ customers competing for every GPU and strong pricing on old and new GPUs. NVIDIA: $2bn equity plus line of sight to 5 GW of capacity by 2030. AI-cloud pipeline 3.5x q/q.
- 2026-08-12 (Q2-26; file dated 2026-08-19): ARR $3bn at end-June (+58% q/q); AI cloud revenue $574.9m (+514% y/y). Q2 capex $5.7bn. 2026 guide reaffirmed: ARR $7–9bn, revenue $3.0–3.4bn, capex $20–25bn. ~$40bn of committed backlog, with more than $9bn of customer prepayments expected in 2026. Year-end contracted power target raised to 5 GW; 800 MW–1 GW connected in 2026; more than 1 GW of new capacity a year from 2027.

Current state (latest call): demand exceeds capacity and prices are rising. The 2026 capex guide stays at $20–25bn, the contracted-power target is 5 GW and committed backlog is ~$40bn, anchored by Meta and Microsoft.
