# ANET - AI demand notes from earnings calls, 2022-02-14 to 2026-08-04
(16 calls in sample; no calls between 2025-02-18 and 2026-02-12, so the Q1-Q3 2025 AI ramp is not directly covered)

- 2022-02-14: Supply-constrained cloud boom. Reiterated ~30% 2022 growth (~$3.85bn) after 2021 revenue of $2.94bn (+27%); lead times "now extending into 2023"; purchase commitments $2.8bn (from $2.1bn), inventory $650.1M; customer price increases. Microsoft the only >10% customer (15%); Meta expected to become the second 10% customer in 2022.
- 2022-08-01: Peak scarcity. Component lead times ~70 weeks, customer lead time 52 weeks ("visibility is roughly a year"); supplier decommits and broker buys; H1'22 growth ~40%; Q3 guide $1.025-1.075bn; Microsoft and Meta each expected >10% of revenue; management committed to double-digit growth in 2023.
- 2022-10-31: Raised 2022 growth to ~45% (from the 30% initial outlook), with cloud titans ~45% of revenue (Meta, Microsoft); inventory $1.1bn; supply "stubbornly constrained"; still expected double-digit growth in 2023.
- 2023-02-13: Peak concentration: Meta 25.5% and Microsoft 16% of 2022 revenue. 2023 outlook ~25% ($5.47bn); purchase commitments fell to $3.7bn (from $4.3bn) as lead times improve, so management expects less visibility.
- 2023-05-01: First cloud-titan warning ("some moderation in customer spending, especially with our cloud titan customers"). Endorsed ~26% 2023 growth (~$5.5bn); said AI would be "meaningful this year" after not being material; 72-week lead times still on many components.
- 2023-07-31: Digestion episode: "One specific cloud [titan] customer has signaled a slowdown in capex"; "difficult to repeat the last two years of exceptional cloud capex". Yet the 2023 outlook was raised to >30%. AI timeline: 2023 is planning, 2024 pilots (4,000-8,000 GPUs), majority in 2025.
- 2023-10-30: 2023 growth raised to ~33% (vs 25% analyst-day forecast); expects moderation of cloud spending into 2024. Created the "cloud and AI titan" segment (>40% of mix), adding Oracle OCI; customers "doubled down on AI" back-end spending.
- 2024-02-12: Set an AI target of at least $750M in AI networking for 2025. The 2024 guide was only 10-12% (~$6.5bn) on moderated cloud spend, after 2023 revenue of $5.86bn (+33.8%). Meta was 21% and Microsoft 18%; Arista won 4 of the last 5 AI cluster bids vs InfiniBand.
- 2024-05-07: Upturn: raised the 2024 guide to 12-14% (Q2 guide $1.62-1.65bn); all 4 AI customers moving from trials to pilots, with 10,000-100,000 GPU production expected in 2025; a cloud titan's 24,000-node GPU cluster runs on the 7800 AI spine.
- 2024-11-07: 2024 heading to at least 18% growth (vs 10-12% prior); 2025 guide 15-17%; AI-centers target ~$1.5bn for 2025 (incl. $750M back-end). Raised purchase commitments for switching silicon (with inventory, $4.1bn total); deferred revenue $2.5bn (product +$320M). The fifth AI customer is "stalled".
- 2025-02-18: 2025 guide raised to ~17% ($8.2bn, from 15-17%); AI $1.5bn in 2025 including $750M back-end; 3 of 4 AI customers to roll out a cumulative 100,000 GPUs in 2025. Microsoft ~20%, Meta 14.6% (Meta's 2023 capex was down 15-20%); Oracle is now a cloud & AI titan. ~$100M Santa Clara capex; purchase commitments $3.1bn.
- 2026-02-12: Demand accelerating while costs rise. 2025 revenue $9bn (+28.6%), AI center networking above $1.5bn. 2026 guide raised to 25% (~$11.25bn); AI goal raised to $3.25bn (from $2.75bn). Memory and silicon shortages have "worsened significantly". Purchase commitments $6.8bn (from $4.8bn), deferred revenue $5.4bn; AMD now 20-25% of AI deployments; the AI and specialty providers segment (Apple, Oracle, neoclouds) is 20% of revenue, cloud & AI titans 48%; 1-2 new 10% customers expected.
- 2026-05-05: Supply-gated quarter: 2026 guide raised to 27.7% ($11.5bn), AI goal $3.5bn. Industry-wide shortages ("wafers, silicon chips, CPUs, optics, and ... memory"), 52-week lead times, with constraints expected for the "next couple of years". Purchase commitments $8.9bn, deferred revenue $6.2bn (product +$643M); the CFO said demand outstripping supply extends beyond Q1/Q2.
- 2026-08-04: First $3bn quarter, and a third raise of the 2026 guide, to $12.6bn (+40%, +$1.1bn over $11.5bn). AI target at least $3.6bn (scale-across ~30%); more than 100 AI fabric customers (vs 4-5 in 2024). Multiyear purchase commitments ~$9.7bn (vs $3.6bn a year earlier); memory secured for 2026; manufacturing and distribution capacity increased; deferred revenue ~$6.9bn.
Current state (latest call): 2026-08-04: demand still exceeds supply, but supply is improving. FY26 guide $12.6bn (+40%), AI fabrics at least $3.5-3.6bn, Q3 guide ~$3.3bn; purchase commitments ~$9.7bn and deferred revenue ~$6.9bn give visibility into 2027; Microsoft and Meta remain the anchors, with 1-2 new 10% customers possible.
