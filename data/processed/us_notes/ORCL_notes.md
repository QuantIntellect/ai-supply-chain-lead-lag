# ORCL — demand notes from earnings calls (2022–2026)

Coverage: 17 calls, 2022-03-10 to 2026-09-10 (actual call dates). Missing: Q4 FY25 (Jun-2025) and Q1 FY26 (Sep-2025), which was the quarter of the first RPO step-change. Fiscal year ends May. Capex is reported capex; land, buildings and power are leased and excluded. From FY27 Oracle also reports a "net cash outlay", which is capex net of customer prepayments.

- 2022-06-13: FY23 capex was guided higher "to meet the demand", with 6 more cloud regions (38 already live). FY23 cloud growth was expected to "accelerate substantially".
- 2022-12-12: IaaS bookings grew at triple digits. Capex was $2.4bn, with about that much per quarter ahead. RPO reached $61.2bn (+68% cc, including Cerner), and FY23 organic cloud growth was guided above 30%.
- 2023-06-12: AI inflection. Generative-AI customers had signed for more than $2bn of OCI capacity amid "unprecedented demand". The FY24 capex projection was raised to roughly FY23's $8.7bn. Oracle is building a 16,000-GPU cluster with NVIDIA. RPO was $67.9bn (+47% cc).
- 2023-09-11: AI training contracts passed $4bn, twice the level at the end of Q4.
- 2023-12-11: "Billions of dollars more in contracted demand than we currently can supply." Oracle is expanding 66 data centers and building 100 new ones, including 20 for Microsoft. FY24 capex was guided to about $8bn, and OCI growth was expected to exceed 50% "for a few years".
- 2024-03-11: Supply constraints held back consumption growth, and FY24 capex was cut to $7–7.5bn. RPO was about $80bn (the transcript says "8 billion"), with the ex-Cerner portion up 41%. At least 40 AI bookings of more than $1bn had not yet come online.
- 2024-06-11: Oracle signed the largest contracts in its history: $12.5bn of AI contracts from 30 customers in Q4 (nearly $17bn for the year). RPO reached $98bn (+44%). OpenAI selected OCI. FY25 capex was guided to about 2x FY24's $6.9bn.
- 2024-09-09 to 2024-12-09: RPO was $99bn (+52%), then $97.3bn. OCI consumption grew 56%, then 58%, with demand "outstrip[ping] supply". GPU consumption rose 336%. Data-center scale grew from 800MW to 1.6GW. FY25 capex was still guided at 2x FY24.
- 2025-03-10: The strongest booking quarter ever added $48bn, taking RPO to $130bn (+63%), before any Stargate contracts. FY25 capex was guided to about $16bn. Component delays were expected to ease in Q1 FY26. Power capacity was expected to double in calendar 2025 and triple by the end of FY26.
- 2025-12-10: RPO reached $523.3bn (+433%, +$68bn since August) on contracts with Meta, NVIDIA and others. OCI grew 66% to $4.1bn, and GPU revenue grew 177%. FY26 capex is about $15bn above the forecast given after Q1 (the transcript is garbled and the Q1 FY26 call is missing). FY27 revenue was guided $4bn higher. Expansion will proceed only "when ... the capital is available on favorable terms".
- 2026-03-10: RPO reached $553bn, and AI-infrastructure revenue grew 243%. GPU and CPU demand "continues to exceed supply". Oracle has secured more than 10GW over three years through partners, with more than 90% partner-funded.
- 2026-06-10: RPO reached $638bn (+363%). Q4 brought $67bn of AI contracts, mostly bring-your-own-hardware or prepaid, taking such contracts to $75bn. OCI grew 93%. FY26 net cash capex was $48bn, and FY27 is guided to about $70bn net cash (about $90–95bn reported). Oracle delivered 1.2GW in FY26 and nearly 1GW in Q1 FY27 alone.
- 2026-09-10: OCI grew 121% to $7.4bn. RPO rose $26bn q/q, mostly prepay or bring-your-own-hardware contracts that hit revenue and capex only in FY28 and later. The FY27 revenue guide was raised to at least $90bn (+34%). GPU renewals repriced 20% higher, and 850MW was delivered since Q4. FY27 capex guidance of $90–95bn reported and no more than $70bn net cash was reiterated.

Current state (latest call, 2026-09-10): OCI growth is accelerating (+121%) on a backlog of about $664bn (implied: $638bn plus $26bn). GPU capacity is renewing at higher prices. Capex is peaking ($90–95bn reported in FY27), but growth is increasingly financed by customer prepayments, bring-your-own-hardware deals and partners. Financing, not demand, is the binding constraint.
