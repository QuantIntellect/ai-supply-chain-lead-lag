# MSFT — demand notes from earnings calls (2022–2026)

Coverage: 17 calls, 2022-01-25 to 2026-07-29 (actual call dates). Missing: Q3 FY23 (Apr-2023) and Q3 FY25 (Apr-2025). Fiscal year ends June. Capex is reported including finance leases.

- 2022-01-25: Commercial bookings grew 32% (+37% in constant currency), "significantly ahead of expectations", on large long-term Azure contracts. Capex was $6.8bn (+25% y/y), below plan on build-out timing, and Q3 capex was guided slightly down q/q.
- 2022-07-26 to 2023-01-24: Digestion. Azure growth slowed from 40% (46% cc) to 35% (42% cc) to 31% (38% cc), the first two quarters about a point below plan on "moderation in Azure consumption growth" as customers optimized. On 2023-01-24 commercial bookings grew only 7% (+4% cc), "lower than expected". Quarterly capex guidance swung between down and up q/q.
- 2023-07-25: AI inflection. Management said "we will accelerate investment in our cloud infrastructure" and guided capex up q/q in every quarter of FY24 (Q4 FY23 actual: $10.7bn). Azure grew 26% (27% cc) with ~1pt from AI, and management warned AI growth "will be gradual".
- 2023-10-24 to 2024-04-25: AI's contribution to Azure grew from ~3pts to 6pts to 7pts, while total Azure growth went 29% → 30% → 31%. Capex went $11.2bn → $11.5bn → $14bn. Q2 FY24 came in below plan because a third-party capacity delivery slipped into Q3, and Q3 and Q4 were guided to "increase materially". On 2024-04-25 bookings grew 29% (+31% cc) on larger and longer Azure commitments, and management said FY25 capex would be higher than FY24.
- 2024-07-30: First explicit capacity constraint. AI added 8pts to Azure growth "where demand remained higher than our available capacity", and constraints were expected to "remain in Q4 and ... in H1". Capex was $19bn, and FY25 capex was guided above FY24.
- 2024-10-30: The AI business was on track to pass a $10bn annual run-rate the following quarter. Azure grew 33% (34% cc) with ~12pts from AI. The Q2 Azure guide of 31–32% was held back by third-party capacity "supply pushouts" into H2. Capex was $20bn.
- 2025-01-29: The AI run-rate passed $13bn (+175% y/y), and AI added 13pts to Azure (AI services +157%) with demand still above capacity. Management then signalled the cycle's only slowdown: supply would be "roughly in line with near-term demand" by June 2025, Q3–Q4 capex would stay flat at Q2's $22.6bn, and the FY26 capex growth rate would be "lower than FY '25".
- 2025-07-30: Reacceleration. Annual commercial bookings passed $100bn for the first time (+30% cc), and commercial RPO reached $368bn (+35% cc). More than 2GW of capacity was stood up in 12 months. The company expected to stay capacity-constrained through December 2025. Q1 FY26 capex was guided above $30bn (Q4 FY25: $24.2bn including $5bn of finance leases).
- 2025-10-29: OpenAI contracted an incremental $250bn of Azure, and bookings rose 112%. Commercial RPO reached $392bn (+51%) and capex was $34.9bn. Management now said FY26 capex growth would be higher than FY25's, reversing the January/July guidance. The constraint was extended to at least June 2026. AI capacity would grow more than 80% that year and the data-center footprint would double in two years, with Fairwater in Wisconsin scaling to 2GW.
- 2026-01-28: Commercial RPO reached $625bn, about 45% of it from OpenAI, and an Anthropic commitment was signed in November. Capex was $37.5bn, about two-thirds on GPUs and CPUs. Nearly 1GW was added in the quarter, and "demand continues to exceed our supply". Rising memory prices were flagged as a capex headwind, and Q3 capex was guided down q/q on finance-lease timing.
- 2026-04-29: Calendar-2026 capex was guided to about $190bn, including about $25bn from higher component prices, with Q4 FY26 above $40bn. The AI run-rate passed $37bn (+123%), another gigawatt was added, and the company expects to stay constrained "at least through 2026". Excluding OpenAI, bookings grew only 7% and RPO 26%.
- 2026-07-29: Commercial RPO reached $678bn (+84%), and all of the q/q increase came from customers other than frontier-model companies. Azure grew 43% and Q1 FY27 was guided to about 45% cc, with demand still above supply. The company added 31 data centers in the quarter (88 in FY26) and another 1GW. Q4 capex was $41bn and Q1 FY27 is guided above $50bn. Calendar-2026 capex was restated to about $175bn only because data-center useful lives were extended from 15 to 25 years, which shifts future data-center leases from finance leases (counted in capex) to operating leases (not counted). The underlying plan is "unchanged", and FY27 capex is expected to grow.

Current state (latest call, 2026-07-29): Demand still exceeds supply, and Azure is accelerating toward about 45%. RPO of $678bn is now growing outside OpenAI. Calendar-2026 capex is about $190bn on the old basis (about $175bn after the lease reclassification), Q1 FY27 is guided above $50bn, FY27 capex is expected to grow, and capacity is expected to stay constrained at least through 2026.
