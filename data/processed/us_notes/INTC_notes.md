# INTC - AI demand notes from earnings calls, 2022-01-26 to 2026-04-23
(17 calls in sample; no Apr-2025 call and no Jul-2026 call, so the latest data point is the 2026-04-23 call)

- 2022-01-26: Peak of the supply-constrained cycle. Q4'21 revenue was $19.5bn and DCG had a record $7.3bn (+20% YoY). "Unprecedented demand" was capped by substrate, component and foundry shortages, which are expected to persist through 2022 into 2023. PSG lost over $500M of 2021 revenue to supply. Q1'22 was guided to $18.3bn, with OEM notebook inventory burn.
- 2022-07-28: Demand break. Q2 revenue of $15.3bn came in 15% below the original guide. The FY22 guide was cut to $65-68bn from $76bn. DCAI fell to $4.6bn (-16% YoY) on OEM inventory reductions and competition, and the PC TAM is seen down about 10%. Net capex was cut to $23bn (-$4bn). NVIDIA picked Sapphire Rapids for DGX H100, and MediaTek signed as a foundry customer.
- 2022-10-27: The slowdown spread from consumer PCs to enterprise ("enterprise demand has begun to slow"). DCAI was $4.2bn (-27% YoY), Q4 was guided to $14-15bn, and net capex was cut by another $2bn.
- 2023-01-26: Trough. Q1'23 was guided to $10.5-11.5bn, with the low end "more likely". 2022 sell-in ran about 10% below consumption, and Q1 is "the most significant inventory digestion in our data set". Server consumption TAM is to fall YoY in 1H23. Sapphire Rapids is ramping against a "strong backlog" toward 1M units by midyear.
- 2023-07-27: Inventory burn easing. CCG was $6.8bn (+18% QoQ) as customer burn slowed. More than 25% of Xeon data-center shipments are "targeted for AI workloads". Q3 was guided to $12.9-13.9bn, while NEX's inventory correction continues into Q3.
- 2023-10-26: Customers finished their inventory burn in 1H23. Gaudi is supply-constrained ("racing to catch up"). A major customer committed to 18A and Intel 3 with a prepayment. Dell (PowerEdge with Gaudi) and Stability AI (4000 Gaudi-2) are AI partners, and TSMC took a minority stake in IMS.
- 2024-01-25: AI pulls server wallet share away from CPUs. Intel has shipped more than 2.5M 4th Gen Xeon, about one-third of them AI-driven, and the accelerator pipeline is above $2bn with Gaudi supply raised. Even so, Q1 was guided to $12.2-13.2bn, with data-center revenue down double digits QoQ amid a "wallet share shift between CPU and accelerators".
- 2024-04-25: Called Q1 the bottom and guided Q2 to $12.5-13.5bn. Expects over $500M of Gaudi accelerator revenue in 2H24. Core Ultra AI PCs are supply-limited by wafer-level assembly. Microsoft was named the fifth Intel 18A customer.
- 2024-08-01: Second demand reset. Q3 was guided to $12.5-13.5bn, and the 2024 TAM was reduced because cloud capex is prioritising AI servers. 2024 gross capex was cut to $25-27bn (more than 20% below plan), and 2025 net capex planned at $12-14bn.
- 2024-10-31: AI accelerator miss. Gaudi will not reach its $500M 2024 target, and Intel took a $300M accelerator inventory write-down. Intel is finalizing a multiyear, multibillion-dollar AWS commitment for a custom Xeon 6 on Intel 3 and an AI fabric chip on 18A. 2025 gross capex is planned at $20-23bn.
- 2025-07-24: Retrenchment. 2025 capex was lowered by about $5bn year-to-date to about $18bn gross. The Germany and Poland fabs were cancelled and Ohio slowed; 14A capex now waits for customer volume commitments. Q3 was guided to $12.6-13.6bn, and Xeon 6 was chosen as host CPU for NVIDIA DGX systems.
- 2025-10-23: Swing to shortage. Capacity limits on Intel 10/7 stopped Intel from fully meeting data-center and client demand. Tight supply is expected to "persist into 2026", with shortages peaking in Q1'26. Intel received $5.7bn from the US government and $2bn from SoftBank, and NVIDIA's $5bn investment (with an NVLink x86 collaboration) is pending.
- 2026-01-22: DCAI reached $4.7bn (+15% QoQ), "the fastest sequential growth this decade", on traditional server demand that hyperscalers say will last "several years". The custom ASIC run-rate passed $1bn (+50% in 2025). Q1'26 was guided to $11.7-12.7bn, held back by supply. 2026 capex was lifted to "flat to down slightly" (from down). NVIDIA's $5bn investment closed, and memory shortages and pricing are now an industry challenge.
- 2026-04-23: Q1 revenue was $13.6bn, $1.4bn above the guide midpoint, and "demand continues to outpace our growing supply". AI-driven businesses are 60% of revenue (+40% YoY), and DCAI was $5.1bn (+22% YoY). Intel signed 3-5 year LTAs, including Google, and joined TeraFab with SpaceX, xAI and Tesla. 2026 capex was moved to flat YoY. Q2 is guided to $13.8-14.8bn, and the PC TAM is seen down low double digits.
Current state (latest call): 2026-04-23. Intel is supply-constrained, with server CPU demand, including AI host CPUs and custom ASICs, outrunning its Intel 10/7 and 18A output. Q2 is guided to $13.8-14.8bn with double-digit industry server CPU unit growth expected. Capex is now flat YoY, versus earlier plans to cut. The main negative is a PC unit TAM down low double digits on memory and component cost inflation.
